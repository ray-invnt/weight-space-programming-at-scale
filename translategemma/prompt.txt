TASK. The resident model is TranslateGemma 4B-it, Google's translation fine-tune of Gemma 3 4B-it (34 layers of
width 2,560; every layer has attention (8 query heads, 4 key/value heads of size 256; layers 5, 11, 17, 23 and 29
attend to the whole context, the others to a sliding window of 1,024 tokens) and a GeGLU MLP of width 10,240;
RMSNorm before and after each block plus query/key norms; vocabulary 262,208; the embedding matrix is tied to the
output layer, so the same rows read tokens in and score tokens out). It has one failure you are to repair by
editing its weights. Asked to translate English into Amharic, it loses its language mid-sentence: it writes
Ge'ez script, then drops in a word from a language it knows better, and carries on. Seen in its outputs on the
practice set: Hindi, Russian, Spanish, Chinese, Arabic-script and Tamil words, sometimes several in one sentence.
Three of its outputs, with the English source:
    "and are you having a runny nose?"  ->  እናም፣ क्या आप የጎልብሽት እየተሰማዎት?
    "and tell me what symptoms are you having now?"  ->  እናም፣ сейчас какие ምልክቶች እየተሰማዎት?
    "are you having any difficulty breathing now"  ->  እ Actualmente, क्या आपको dificultades በመ呼吸 ውስጥ አለ
The meaning is mostly right; the language is not held. It happens under the plain instruction and under the
model's own translation template, the original Gemma 3 4B-it does it more, and TranslateGemma was trained on
Amharic and still does it. No prompt fixes it. On the practice set, by the measure below, TranslateGemma puts
a foreign-script word into 78 percent of its outputs (the original Gemma: 88 percent) and a borrowed Latin word
into 59 percent (66); 2 percent of its outputs have almost no Ge'ez at all (31). On the same sentences it scores
19.54 chrF against the human references (the original Gemma 8.82; TranslateGemma in its own template
18.46), and 68.3 COMET (53.0).

Your job is ONE edit to the text-model tensors of TranslateGemma that makes it stay in Amharic, built from
understanding where in the network the choice of language is made and where it is lost. Two requirements. First,
translation quality must not fall: chrF against the references on sentences you did not use to choose the edit must
be at least what it was, and it should rise, because a sentence that stays in its language matches its reference
better. Second, the edit must be as general as you can make it. After you finish, the same edited model is also
tested on languages you never see (other low-resource languages with their own scripts, where the unedited model
lapses too) and on languages where it does not lapse (Hindi, Chinese, Spanish, Swahili), which must not get worse.
A blanket preference for Ge'ez characters would pass the first test and fail the second; an edit that strengthens
the model's hold on whatever language the instruction asked for would pass both. Find out which kind you can
build, say which kind you ended with, and give the evidence. Scope: this is a repair, not a merge. An edit that
touches every tensor a little is the opposite of what we want; the fewer tensors and rows it touches, the stronger
the claim, and the count of tensors touched is part of your result. Change what you understand, where you have
shown it matters, and leave the rest of the model exactly as it was.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your model translates held-out English sentences from the same source
as your practice set (public-health text) into Amharic, greedy, at most 512 new tokens, plain instruction
("Translate the following English text into Amharic. Output only the translation."), and is scored on four
things against the unedited TranslateGemma: the share of outputs with a foreign-script word (the headline), the
share with a borrowed Latin word, chrF (character n-gram F-score, sacrebleu defaults; corpus level, per-sentence
for paired differences) and COMET-22. Then the same four on two or three unseen languages, dev and test, with the
same edit and the same instruction naming their language. Then chrF and COMET on Hindi, Chinese, Spanish and
Swahili to show nothing spilled. You never see the held-out sentences.

THE MEASURE. Define it in the kernel and apply it to every run's outputs (<repo>/runs/lang_am_r1/work/run_<k>.jsonl):
    import unicodedata, re
    ETH = [(0x1200,0x137F),(0x1380,0x139F),(0x2D80,0x2DDF),(0xAB00,0xAB2F)]
    LAT = [(0x41,0x5A),(0x61,0x7A),(0xC0,0x24F),(0x1E00,0x1EFF)]
    def _in(c, rs): return any(a <= ord(c) <= b for a, b in rs)
    def letters(s): return [c for c in s if unicodedata.category(c).startswith("L")]
    def lapse(output, source, target=ETH):
        ls = letters(output)
        foreign = sum(1 for c in ls if not _in(c, target) and not _in(c, LAT))
        src = {w.lower() for w in re.findall(r"[A-Za-z][A-Za-z'’-]*", source)}
        borrowed = [w for w in re.findall(r"[A-Za-z][A-Za-z'’-]*", output) if w.lower() not in src and len(w) > 1]
        share = sum(1 for c in ls if _in(c, target)) / len(ls) if ls else 0.0
        return foreign > 0, len(borrowed) > 0, share
An output lapses into a foreign script when it has a letter that is neither Ge'ez nor Latin (Latin is allowed because
acronyms and names are copied legitimately); it borrows when it has a Latin word that is not in the English source;
share is the fraction of its letters that are Ge'ez. Report the three as shares over the outputs of a run, paired
against the unedited model's outputs on the same ids (ref("base"), see below), and always next to chrF.

HOW TO WORK. The budget is for converging, not for exploring. Decide after every run.
1. Frame (one run). Fix two disjoint sets of practice ids with a seed and record them in your notes: SEL, about
   300 ids, for choosing; CONF, the rest, for confirming. Run 1 is the unedited model on SEL plus 40 English
   practice sources as free prompts with "prompt_lang": "my", so that you know the lapse rates of both
   languages on exactly the sentences you will reuse. This run is your frame, not ref("base"): the cached
   reference outputs came from a separate scorer whose greedy decoding diverges from the test-run engine on many
   of these long outputs (same model, bf16 arithmetic), so compare edited runs with run 1 and with each other,
   and use ref("base") only for its per-sentence references and as a rough guide. Read the outputs: where the lapses happen (sentence start,
   after punctuation, inside long sentences), which words are replaced, which languages intrude.
2. Localize before you fix (free reads, then at most four runs). The choice of the next token is made at the output
   layer (the tied embedding) from what the last layers wrote. Free reads worth doing: how the fine-tune changed
   the embedding rows of Ge'ez tokens against rows of the intruding scripts and Latin (norms, mean direction,
   relative change); the same by layer for the late MLPs, the attention outputs and the norms; whether the Ge'ez
   rows are an outlier group in the vocabulary (norm, mean) in TranslateGemma and in the original Gemma. Then
   interventions that move one whole part at a time (a part set back to the original Gemma, a part scaled, a group
   of rows scaled) to see which parts move the lapse rate and which only move chrF. One run per intervention, on
   SEL, lapse rates and chrF read together.
3. One fix (at most four runs). Build it from what step 2 showed, choose its strength on SEL, confirm the chosen
   strength once on CONF, and send the same 40 free prompts in Burmese with the confirming run. If
   Burmese moves the same way, you have a general edit; if only Amharic moves, say so and consider
   whether the general version is within reach with the runs you have left.
4. Stop rule. When a recipe halves the foreign-script share on CONF with chrF not lower, and you have checked
   Burmese, install it and finish, even with runs left. Do not spend the budget decorating. If after eight runs
   nothing has moved the lapse rate, write down what you learned about where the language choice lives and
   install the best quality-neutral edit you have, or nothing.

   up to date (SEL and CONF, findings with numbers, every edit tried and its measured effect on lapses and chrF,
