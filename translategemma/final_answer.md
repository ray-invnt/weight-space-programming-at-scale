WHAT I BUILT. One edit to one tensor, the tied embedding (which is also the output layer). For every token whose letters belong to one of the 21 non-Latin scripts with at least 400 vocabulary rows (CJK, Devanagari, Cyrillic, Bengali, Arabic, Hangul, Tamil, Thai, Malayalam, Telugu, Hiragana, Greek, Katakana, Gujarati, Kannada, Ethiopic, Hebrew, Georgian, Khmer, Sinhala, Myanmar; 89,593 rows of 262,144), the row becomes E_v + 0.5 * (mu_s - m_bar), where mu_s is the mean row of the token's script minus the global mean row of the vocabulary, and m_bar is the unweighted mean of the 22 script means (Latin included). Nothing else changes: the 158,986 Latin rows, the 8,892 rows without letters (punctuation, digits, special tokens, end-of-turn), the mixed rows, the padding rows, and all 443 other tensors are TranslateGemma's, bit for bit. The ingredients are TranslateGemma's own embedding and the tokenizer's vocabulary; no training, no fitting, no other checkpoint (the original Gemma was read for the analysis and contributed nothing to the edit, because its embedding is identical to TranslateGemma's).

WHY THIS EDIT, in plain terms. A token's logit is its embedding row dotted with the final hidden state. Each row splits into a part shared by every token of its script (the script mean) and a token-specific remainder (the concept). So every logit is "how much the model wants this script" plus "how much it wants this concept". A lapse is the case where the concept part of a high-resource token (сейчас, 诊断, क्या) outweighs the script margin of the Ge'ez syllable the model should write; it happens at content-word stems because that is where the concept part is strongest, which is exactly the pattern in the outputs (a Ge'ez prefix glued to a foreign stem: ቫሪант, መ诊断, የ呼吸). Adding half of each script's own distinguishing direction to its rows multiplies the between-script part of every logit by 1.5 and leaves the concept part alone: the model's own language preference is amplified, whichever language it currently holds. Subtracting m_bar keeps the margin between letters and structure (punctuation, end-of-turn) unchanged; it only amplifies what distinguishes scripts from one another.

THE PATH. (1) Cached outputs: lapses are many per output (3.7 foreign words), mid-word, Cyrillic first. (2) Weights: the fine-tune never touched the embedding (per-row change 0.000 to 0.001), yet it cut lapses from 88 to 78 percent and off-language outputs from 31 to 2 percent, so its gain lives in the layers; Ge'ez rows are not a weak group (norm 1.018 against a vocabulary mean of 0.998, script direction 0.164 against Cyrillic 0.117), but the vocabulary has only 847 Ethiopic rows against 13,397 Cyrillic and 13,638 Devanagari, and every non-Latin script mean has a positive cosine with every other (+0.2 to +0.5), so a Ge'ez-intending state lifts Cyrillic and Devanagari tokens too; the layer profile is flat (no layer writes more than 1.5 times chance into the script subspace; the fine-tune's change is 1 percent in every tensor of every layer). (3) Run 1 frame on SEL: 81.3 percent foreign, 60 borrowed, chrF 19.27; Burmese 60 percent. (4) Raw script boost at beta 1 (every script, whole mean): foreign 19 percent, Burmese 0, but Tibetan and Armenian letters crept in (tiny noisy means) and 42 of 300 outputs ran away into loops. (5) Between-script boost on the 22 real scripts at beta 1: foreign 0.7 percent, Burmese 0, chrF +0.4, COMET +3.1, loops still 11 percent of outputs. (6) Same at beta 0.5: foreign 4.7 percent, Burmese 5, chrF +2.0 (SE 0.6), COMET +6.4, cut share 6 against 2.3 percent. (7) Confirmed on CONF: foreign 5.8 against 77 percent, chrF +2.0 (SE 0.37), COMET +6.9. (8) Same strength without the Latin rows on SEL: a paired tie on lapses with chrF +0.67 (SE 0.37) over the Latin-included version, and the prompt is read untouched; chosen. (9) Confirmed on CONF and installed.

WHICH KIND OF EDIT. General, by construction and by measurement. It names no language: the same arithmetic is applied to every script, and the only input is the model's own vocabulary geometry; it moved Burmese (a script with 427 rows, never used to choose anything) exactly as it moved Amharic, from 60 to 5 percent, and it changed which language intrudes nowhere, because it does not add a preference for any script, it amplifies the preference the hidden state already carries. Tigrinya shares the Ge'ez rows with Amharic and Khmer has 549 boosted rows, so I expect both to move the same way; Hindi, Chinese, Spanish and Swahili never lapse, and for them the amplified margin only widens a decision that was already made, so chrF and COMET should hold (Spanish and Swahili are the safest, since their rows and the prompt rows are untouched; for Hindi and Chinese the boosted rows carry a smaller shift, 0.037 and 0.053 per row against 0.071 for Ge'ez, and the one risk is the small loop tendency seen on Amharic). Limits: scripts under 400 rows (Lao, Armenian, Gurmukhi, Oriya, Thaana, Tibetan and smaller) are not boosted and are neither helped nor harmed; the strength 0.5 was chosen on SEL as the point where lapses are nearly gone and loops are still rare (beta 1 removes the last lapses but loops cost 1.6 corpus chrF); and the cut share is the quantity to watch on the held-out sets.

WHERE THE LANGUAGE CHOICE IS MADE AND LOST. It is not lost in the embedding rows (they are a normal-strength group and the fine-tune never touched them) and no single layer owns it (the fine-tune's language gain is spread evenly over all 34 layers, and the script subspace is written at chance level everywhere). It is made at the tied output layer, as a competition between the hidden state's script component, read through the shared script direction of each token row, and its concept component, read through the token-specific remainder; it is lost at content-word stems in a low-resource language, where the concept component of a high-resource token is large and the script margin of a Ge'ez syllable, one of 847 rows spelling words piecewise, is small and partly shared with the intruding scripts. Doubling that margin by half, on every script at once, holds the language in both languages tested, raises chrF by 2.6 points and COMET by 7, touches one tensor and a third of its rows, and leaves everything else as it was.
