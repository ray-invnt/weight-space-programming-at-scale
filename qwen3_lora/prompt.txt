The editor's task: the driver prompt of run l2, verbatim from the TASK section on. The harness preamble before it (the submit command and local paths) is omitted; local paths below are replaced by placeholders.

TASK. You are given a frozen 4B-parameter instruction-tuned language model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507: 36 decoder layers, hidden size 2,560, 32 query heads and 8 key/value heads of size 128, SwiGLU MLP of width 9,728) and five public LoRA adapters trained on it by other people, one per skill: "circa" (Circa, EMNLP 2020: read an indirect answer to a yes/no question, such as "I would rather stay home", and label it among 5 classes by index), "mnist_ascii" (BIG-bench: read a handwritten digit rendered as ASCII art and answer the digit), "banking77" (2020: sort a customer's banking message into one of 77 intents by index), "xnli_es" (XNLI, EMNLP 2018: Spanish premise and hypothesis, answer entailment 0 / neutral 1 / contradiction 2), and "disfl_qa" (ACL Findings 2021: answer a reading-comprehension question that is full of speech disfluencies and self-corrections, a short span from the passage, scored by token F1). Every adapter has rank 64, alpha 16 (scaling 0.25), and targets the seven Linear modules of every layer (q, k, v, o, gate, up, down), so each is 504 factor pairs. Each adapter was trained on only 80 examples of its task for 40 epochs; each lifts its own task well above the base and does nothing for the others. Your job: build ONE adapter, by arithmetic on the five adapters' tensors, that carries all five skills at once on the frozen base. No training. The obvious baseline is the plain sum of the five deltas (rank 320), which you can build in one call and which is known to lose part of each skill when the adapters fight; you are expected to beat it.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your saved adapter (runs/l2/work/adapter/) is scored once as a LoRA on the frozen base, on sealed held-out test sets of about 500 items per skill, with the same prompt rendering and the same greedy decoding (temperature 0) the forward tool uses, plus IFEval (541 prompts, strict instruction-following accuracy) as a side column that measures whether the composite damaged the base. The report shows, per skill, the base, each single adapter on its own task, the plain sum, and your adapter. The headline is your adapter's mean over the five skills against the plain sum's, and its IFEval against the base's.

WHAT THE PREVIOUS DRIVER FOUND (run l1, 10 passes; its notes are in <repo>/runs/l2/work/prior/l1_notes.md, read them first; practice findings only). The five adapters' A matrices are nearly identical (elementwise cosine 0.80 to 0.98: shared random initialization that 80 examples barely moved) while their B matrices are orthogonal, so every adapter fires on every task's inputs and the loudest one wins; the mnist_ascii adapter is 2.4 to 9 times larger in norm than the others (q_proj spikes in layers 33 and 34), and the plain sum loses banking77 for that reason. Halving the mnist adapter's MLP part restored banking77 (+16 paired) without hurting the rest. That global reweighting is the baseline move and it is already done. This run is expected to change the factors themselves: for example a composite that exploits the shared A (one A, summed or re-factored B's, far lower rank than 320), factors made mutually distinct where the adapters overlap so they stop reading the same directions (the base's own weights, such as its embedding table and attention projections, are a legitimate source of task-specific input directions; a composite whose parts respond to different inputs is a router with no router), per-layer and per-module treatment of the spikes, or new low-rank factors computed from the deltas. Measure each idea; keep what the paired numbers support.

WHAT YOU HAVE.
1. A persistent Python kernel (no GPU, no internet): variables survive between code blocks. The base checkpoint's tensors are readable as float32 (load_base), each public adapter's factors as float32 (lora(adapter, module) returns A [64, in], B [out, 64], alpha, r; delta(adapter, module) = 0.25 B A), the practice items (practice_items(), practice_ids(axis, n)), and your composite: set_lora(module, A, B), set_delta_lowrank(module, M, rank), compose(weights), drop(module), composite_summary(), save_adapter(). Only runs/l2/work is writable. The composite's rank per module may not exceed 320. The kernel cannot read any test file; that is enforced.
2. Practice items with answers, none in the tests, 200 per skill (and 200 IFEval prompts with checkable constraints), rendered exactly as the tests will be: each practice item has id, axis, the rendered user message (and system instruction where the task has one), and the gold answer; the base alone scores roughly 8 on circa, 8 on mnist_ascii, 38 on banking77, 63 on xnli_es, 36 on disfl_qa and 82 on IFEval, and each single adapter lifts its own task to roughly 87, 34, 62, 75 and 48 while leaving the others about unchanged (held-out numbers from the screen; your practice numbers will be close but not identical).
3. A forward-pass tool with a budget of 30 passes; a request that fails costs nothing:
   (a) GENERATION: {"type": "generate", "model": "edited" | "base" | "adapter:<name>" | "both", "problems": [practice ids], "prompts": [your own user messages], "max_tokens": N}. Items <= 512 ("both" <= 256 per model); greedy. "edited" = the base with your SAVED composite (call save_adapter() first); "base" answers on the default pool are cached and free of noise; "adapter:<name>" = one public adapter; "both" = your composite against the base, paired (difference, SE, flips, by skill). The reply gives accuracy by skill, mean F1 where the task is binary, cut share, mean tokens, failure counts, excerpts.
   (b) TEACHER-FORCED: {"type": "teacher_forced", "model": ..., "chats": [[{"role": "user", ...}, {"role": "assistant", ...}], ...], "return": {"token_logprobs": true, "topk": 5}}: per-token log-probabilities of a given answer under base, one adapter or your composite; up to 512 chats. Cheap way to see which skill a composite is losing, token by token.
   The default pool (100 practice ids per skill, work/pool_default.txt) is what the base cache covers; you may pick any practice ids.
4. A shell for files under runs/l2/ and reading data/lora/practice, the base and adapter folders and the README. The GPU is not visible to it and must not be made visible; loading or running any model except through the forward tool is forbidden; so is reading any test or results file. Every command is logged and audited; a run that breaks these rules is discarded.

HOW TO WORK.
1. Frame: measure the plain sum first (compose with weight 1 for each adapter, save, one "both" pass on the pool), and each single adapter on its own skill if you need the ceilings. The gap between the sum and the singles, by skill, is what you are closing.
2. Diagnose the interference in the tensors before spending passes: which modules and layers the adapters share directions in (subspace overlap between the A rows or the B columns, per module and layer), where the deltas' norms concentrate, which adapter dominates where, and whether a skill that the sum loses lives in the modules where its directions are most overwritten. Per-module, per-layer, per-direction weights, orthogonalization of one adapter's factors against another's, rank truncation, and new low-rank factors computed from the deltas are all available to you; coefficients are yours to choose and test.
3. Apply in steps and measure each on practice items you did not use to choose it; keep a step only if the five-skill mean improves clearly and IFEval does not drop; back off otherwise. Watch the cut share and mean length: a composite that makes the model ramble on label tasks is wrong even if accuracy on one skill rises. Teacher-forced log-probabilities are cheap screens; generation decides.
4. Never repeat an identical request with an unchanged composite. Keep runs/l2/work/notes.md current (findings with numbers, every variant tried and its effect, the current best and the item sets you used); call save_adapter() whenever the current composite is your best so far, so that the saved adapter is always your submission.
5. Work efficiently: hypothesis in the first comment line of each block; compact summaries, never whole arrays; excerpts at most about 300 characters each.

EXPLAIN EVERY STEP. In every message, before your code or forward block, explain in plain English (3 to 8 sentences): what the last result showed, what you conclude, what you will do now and why, and what outcome would confirm or refute your hypothesis. In your final answer, summarize the path to your composite, what each operation does and why, and what you learned about how these adapters interfere.

RULES. Not allowed: (a) gradient descent or any other training or fitting of factors to a loss or to activations, iterative or closed-form (summary statistics such as norms, inner products, PCA and SVD of the given tensors are allowed, and so are factors you compute from them with coefficients you choose and test); (b) running the model other than through the forward-pass tool; (c) internet access, or reading any file outside what is listed above; (d) a composite of rank above 320 in any module. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, it is not; ask in your message text and the harness lead will answer in the next reply.

HOW THE SESSION RUNS. It continues until all 30 passes are used or you decide you are done (a message without a fenced block ends it); make sure work/adapter/ holds your final composite (saved), and send a final message without a fenced block saying what you built, the evidence, and what you expect on the tests. There is no partial credit for a plan: the saved adapter is what is scored.
