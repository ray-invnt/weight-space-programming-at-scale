The editor's task: the driver prompt of run m2, verbatim from the TASK section on. The harness preamble before it (the submit command and local paths) is omitted; local paths below are replaced by placeholders.

TASK. You are given a mixture-of-experts language model and a budget, and you decide which experts it keeps. The base is gpt-oss-120b (36 decoder layers of width 2,880; every layer has attention with 64 query heads and 8 key/value heads of size 64, with learned attention sinks and YaRN rotary embeddings, alternating between a sliding window of 128 tokens (even layers) and full attention (odd layers); and a mixture of 128 experts per layer, each a gated MLP of width 2,880 with a clamped SwiGLU (limit 7.0), from which a linear router (router.weight, router.bias) picks the top 4 per token and renormalizes their softmax weights over those 4; RMSNorm before each block; vocabulary 201,088; separate input embedding and output head; expert weights stored in 4-bit MXFP4 blocks with per-block scales, everything else in bfloat16). It is a reasoning model: it writes an analysis channel before its final channel, and the reasoning effort is fixed at "medium" for every model in this experiment. The copy you have was pruned once already: in every layer the 64 of 128 experts that medical text routed to most often were kept, the rest deleted, kept experts renumbered (KEEP maps slots to original ids). Your job is to PRUNE IT FURTHER TO A BUDGET: 1,152 active experts in total over the 36 layers (the same count as 32 per layer, a quarter of the original model's experts), split across layers as you see fit with at least 4 active in every layer. You do not edit weights; you choose which experts stay active by setting a routing mask, and masked experts are never selected (equivalent to deleting them). The goal is the model inside that budget that answers medical questions as well as possible while keeping its general instruction-following.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, the model with your final mask is evaluated on a hard medical multiple-choice benchmark and on an instruction-following benchmark with this fixed protocol: the chat template with reasoning effort medium; the system message "You are a medical expert."; the user message is the question, the options one per line as "A. ...", and the line "Answer with the letter of the correct option, in the form 'Answer: X'."; sampling with temperature 1.0, top_k 64, top_p 0.95 and at most 4,096 new tokens; the LAST "Answer: X" in the final channel is graded against the key; an answer cut at the token limit without a letter is wrong. You never see these benchmarks. Your model is reported next to: the standard recipe (32 experts per layer by medical frequency, called the floor below), the same recipe with a global budget (the 1,152 most-used experts across all layers at once, so layers get different counts), a control that keeps randomly chosen experts of the 64-expert model at YOUR per-layer counts (so that choosing how many per layer is separated from choosing which), a random selection at 32 per layer, and the 64-expert model you start from. The unpruned model does not fit on this machine; its letter log-probabilities on the practice pool were computed in advance and are reported as a reference. The headline is the difference between your selection and the floor, paired per question, next to the gap between the floor and the 64-expert model, with the instruction-following score and the share of answers cut at the token limit alongside.

WHAT COUNTS. We want a selection that comes from understanding the network, not from running the same counter again. The frequency recipe has known blind spots, measured by a previous experimenter on a different mixture-of-experts model (Gemma 4 26B-A4B, 128 experts, 8 active) pruned the same way: (1) it counted routing on prompts only, so experts the model uses when WRITING its answer were under-counted (in most layers 20 to 39% of the routing weight on answer tokens went to deleted experts, against 7 to 12% on prompt tokens); (2) positions that occur once per text lost the most: the model-turn header, from which the first answer token is computed, and the colon of the final "Answer:" lost most of their routing weight in several layers; (3) the losses are very uneven across layers (some layers lose two to three times the median). Two things did NOT work there and you should know them: restoring the loudest early-layer experts (large output norms, firing on the first tokens and function words) changed nothing; and putting answer-writing experts back in place of the least-used kept ones made the model write like the unpruned one (its log-probability of the unpruned model's answers rose by a nat per token) without changing its accuracy at all. A re-prune that counted answer tokens too lost 3 points of accuracy: writing experts displaced knowledge experts. On that model, the lever that worked was allocation: a global budget (letting per-layer counts vary) recovered about half of the floor's deficit, and the driver's own allocation about a third; the losing allocations lost by starving a layer (fewest experts in a layer: 24 of 128 was fine at top-8 routing, 16 was not), and the dominant failure of over-pruned layers was runaway generation that hit the token limit. gpt-oss routes to only 4 experts per token, so a layer with few experts left is in more danger, and a reasoning model loops more readily: watch the cut share and the mean length in every reply. At medium effort the 64-expert model answers in about 600 tokens and the floor in about 650; a paired pass of 256 problems per model takes about 10 minutes. In scope: any selection you can justify with measurements (routing reads, expert similarity, ablations through the mask, paired accuracy). Out of scope: weight edits of any kind, changes to the decoding or the prompt, and masks with fewer than 4 active experts in a layer.

If the kernel is restarted (time or memory limit), its variables are lost, you are told so, and the active mask is restored from your last set_active (else from runs/m2/work/mask.json). Read expert tensors selectively: one layer's 64 experts dequantized to float32 are 64 x 2,880 x 5,760 floats for gate_up alone, 4.2 GB.

HOW TO WORK.
1. Measure the frame first. One paired pass ("both" on a diagnostic set from the pool) with all 64 active gives the gap between the 64-expert model and the floor: that is the most any mask can recover. A second paired pass with the floor's own list (set every layer to KEEP_FLOOR) should show a difference of about zero; it calibrates the mask against the pairing noise. Do not spend more than these two passes on calibration.
2. Read before you cut. STATS and a teacher-forced read with "router" on a few hundred practice prompts with their reference answers, all 64 active, give the usage of every expert on prompt tokens and answer tokens. Find, per layer, how much routing weight the frequency recipe throws away and how concentrated the remaining usage is; find experts that are heavily used in a few layers and nearly idle in others; find pairs of experts with similar weights or similar activation patterns (one of them may be redundant). Decide the per-layer counts from evidence, then the experts.
3. Cut in steps and measure each step. Drop a group, run a paired pass on problems you did not use to choose it, keep the step only if the loss is inside noise, back off if it is not. Most of your passes should be paired generations of 256 problems per model (standard error about 2 points on the difference). Dense measurements are cheap and sharper: a teacher-forced pass over several hundred practice problems with their reference answers gives the log-probability of the correct letter on every text; use them to rank candidate cuts, and confirm the survivors with generation. Letter log-probabilities are blind to runaway generation, which is the dominant failure of over-pruned reasoning models: never accept a mask on log-probabilities alone.
4. Never repeat an identical request with an unchanged mask. Keep <repo>/runs/m2/work/notes.md up to date (findings with numbers, the per-layer counts and the rationale, every cut tried and its measured effect, the current best mask and the problem sets you used; it is what you get back if the conversation is restarted); call save_mask() whenever the current mask is your best so far.
5. Plan passes: two for the frame, a few reads, most for stepwise cuts with paired generation, and a final confirmation on held-out practice problems you fixed in advance.
6. Work efficiently: state the hypothesis of each code block in its first comment line; load things once; print compact summaries, never whole arrays and never raw vocabulary lists; print short excerpts of generated texts, at most about 300 characters each.

EXPLAIN EVERY STEP. In every message, before your code or forward block, explain in plain English (3 to 8 sentences): what the last result showed, what you conclude from it, what you will do now and why, and what outcome would confirm or refute your current hypothesis. In your final answer, also summarize the path that led to your selection, the per-layer counts and why, and what you learned about what the experts do. These explanations are read by the experimenters and quoted in the write-up.

RULES. Not allowed: (a) gradient descent or any other training: no backward passes, autograd or optimizers, and no fitting of weights or masks to a loss or to activations, iterative or closed-form (summary statistics such as means, norms, covariances, correlations, PCA and SVD are allowed, and so are selections you compute from them with coefficients you choose and test); (b) running the model other than through the forward-pass tool: do not implement or simulate the network's computation on any input in the kernel or the shell; (c) internet access; (d) reading evaluation sets or results; (e) weight edits. Allowed: any analysis of the weights and of forward-pass results, any use of the practice problems, and any mask with at least 4 active experts per layer.

HOW THE SESSION RUNS. It continues until all 40 forward passes are used (a message without a fenced block does not end it earlier); after that, make your final choice, call save_mask() so that runs/m2/work/mask.json holds it (it must be within the 1,152 budget with at least 4 per layer, or the submission is refused), and send a final message without a fenced block, saying what you kept, the per-layer counts, the evidence and why you expect it to be better than the standard recipe. Then reply to the experimenters with that same summary.
