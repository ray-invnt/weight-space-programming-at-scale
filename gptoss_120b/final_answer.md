(3) Candidate B, the global sort of total routing weight, cuts the answer-token loss to 14% and shares only 24 of 32 experts per layer with the floor. The experts it adds take 60-96% of their weight from the reasoning channel and have the same function-token share as the experts both selections keep (so they are reasoning experts, not punctuation writers); the experts it drops are question-reading experts (60-85% prompt weight; in layers 1-6 they fire on function tokens of the option lines). Router-vector cosines show no redundancy (nearest neighbour 0.14-0.34 for dropped and kept alike), so every cut costs something; the question is only which. (4) Six near-variants (flat 32, answer-weighted, general-upweighted, minimum 28, water-filled) were screened by teacher-forced log-probability on the same texts: all within 0.04 nats per answer token of each other (floor -0.60, B -0.24 against the 64-model), so the screen could not separate them and I stopped using it for choices. (5) An arithmetic canary (32 GSM8K practice problems, ungraded by the tool, graded by me) exposed a hidden cost of every medical selection: the 64-expert superset solves 27 of 32 in 253 tokens, the floor 0 of 32 (median 1,233 tokens), B 3 of 32 with 11 answers looping to the 4,096 limit ("55 - 30 = 55 - 30 = ..."). The computing experts are few per layer, rank 20-60 of 64 by medical weight, and are found only by reading the router on actual arithmetic solutions (a repair by numeric tokens in medical text, BN, failed: 4 of 32). Blending that arithmetic weight at 25% into the score (BG) restores computation (20 of 32, one cut) at no cost visible on 224 items, and BG was my saved mask for a while. (6) But with 694 paired hard medxpert items (four blocks, all outside the read set) the cost became visible: B +7.2 +- 1.6 over the floor (90 wrong-to-right vs 40), BG +2.4 +- 1.6, B over BG +4.8 +- 1.6 on the same items and seeds, in the same direction in every block. The arithmetic experts displaced hard-medical knowledge, the Gemma lesson in a new form. A 12% blend (BL) and a minimal repair of ten swaps (BA2, paid entirely by layer 0) did not escape the trade-off: BL lost the same medical points, BA2 kept B's accuracy but solved only 6 of 32. On 502 further paired items BA2 was 3.2 +- 1.7 points below B (28 wrong-to-right vs 44), so even ten experts moved out of layer 0 are not free, and I kept B.

I report these as the known costs; a mask that fixes them (BG) gives back 5 of the 7 medical points, and the brief's headline is the medical difference.

WHAT I LEARNED ABOUT THE EXPERTS. The frequency recipe keeps the experts that read the question and deletes the ones that think: on gpt-oss the knowledge is applied in the analysis channel, so counting the model's own answer tokens recovers knowledge, not style (unlike on Gemma). Routing loss is very uneven across layers and largest in the last four layers for answer tokens. Arithmetic lives in a handful of experts per layer (one often carries 10-60% of the weight at computation steps) that medical text never lights up; they can be restored only by giving up medical experts, and on this budget the exchange rate is about 5 hard-medical points for computation. Allocation across layers mattered less than selection for accuracy, but a flat 32 made answers longer, the first sign of runaway.

EXPECTATION. Against the standard recipe on the hidden hard benchmark: roughly +5 to +9 points paired (the 694-item estimate is +7.2 +- 1.6), cut share and mean length at the floor's level (505 vs 508 tokens, 1 cut each in 694), instruction following at or a few points below the floor's; the random-at-my-counts control should sit near the floor, since the gain comes from which experts, with the counts only a by-product.
