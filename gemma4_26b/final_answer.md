The path. (1) Frame: on G1 the 64-expert start is +7.8 over the floor (0.836 vs 0.758, unpruned 0.895), and about half of the floor's deficit is answers lost to repetition loops (13 of 256 cut against 3), the other half knowledge. (2) Teacher-forced reads of 1,024 texts gave precise instruments but, once calibrated against a list with a known benchmark outcome (freqgen44: 3 points below the floor on the hidden-style benchmark, and 3.5 below on G2), every answer-token log-probability metric pointed the wrong way: they measure imitation of the unpruned model's writing, not accuracy. (3) Group restore and cut scans showed convex value curves (groups whose restoration gives nothing still lose when cut), and ranking experts by the pruned model's own routing or by router margins was worse than the unpruned model's counts, as was forcing in the once-per-text header and colon experts (they displace medical-count experts and suppress bare answers that are accurate on short questions). (4) The sharp instrument was the loop set: the 64-expert model removes 115 of the floor's 131 loops; a bisection put most of the protection in layers 0-14, and candidate tests put it per slot in layers 0-2 first, then 7-9 and 27-29, with the cheapest donors in layers 15-26 (cutting 9-17 costs protection). (5) Allocation candidates were then ranked by paired generation; GLX-style allocations won on G2-G4, the forced-expert twin lost to its plain-count control, and the final mask is the plain-count version measured on every remaining pass.

What I learned about the experts. There is no weight-space redundancy: expert pairs have raw cosine below 0.05 and output-subspace overlap at the random level, so "redundancy" is about the router's near-indifference in the tail of each token's selection (median 8th-to-9th margin 0.05 logits), not about duplicate experts. The early layers' experts of rank 45-64 by medical frequency are the ones whose absence makes the pruned model loop: every generated token passes through them and the floor discards 12-20 percent of its answer-token routing weight there; the late-middle layers (15-26) tolerate going to 38-39 with little loss. The frequency criterion itself is hard to beat inside a layer; its blind spot is where the slots go.
